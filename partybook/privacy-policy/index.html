PartyBook privacy policy

What the app stores, where it is stored, and who can see it.

Last updated 2 September 2026

PartyBook is made by DevRocks, a sole proprietorship in India. Questions about anything on this page go to partybook.support@gmail.com.

Two different people are involved

That distinction runs through everything below, so it is worth stating first.

  • The business using PartyBook — a hotel or caterer, and its staff, who hold accounts we issued.
  • That business's own customers — the people whose names, phone numbers and delivery addresses staff type into an order. These people have no account and never use the app.

For the second group, the business decides what is collected and why; we only hold it for them. If you are one of a shop's customers and want your details corrected or removed, ask the shop — they control that record. We will help them do it, but we will not change another business's records on a third party's instruction.

What is stored

About the business
Hotel name, its hotel code, and optionally an address and phone number. Per branch: name, address, phone number and bill-number prefix.
About staff
A username, a display name, a role (administrator or labour), and whether the account is active. Optionally a list of employee names, so an order can record who took it.
About the menu
Food item names and rates.
About each order
The customer's name, phone number, an optional second phone number and a delivery address. The items and quantities, rates and total, the delivery date and sitting, when it was booked and by which account, amounts paid and how, and — if cancelled — who cancelled it, when, why and any refund.

PINs are not stored. A six-digit PIN never reaches our records in a readable form. What is stored is a value derived from it using PBKDF2 with a per-account salt and 100,000 rounds, which is one-way: nobody, including us, can read a PIN back out of it. That is why a forgotten PIN is replaced rather than recovered.

The branch cancellation code is stored the same way for staff to check against, alongside a copy that only that hotel's own administrators can read — so the owner can tell their staff what it is.

Where it is stored

On Google Firebase — Firestore for records, Firebase Authentication for sign-in credentials, and Cloud Functions for the one operation the app is not permitted to do itself.

The database and those functions are hosted in Google's asia-south1 region, in Mumbai, India. Your records do not leave the country in normal operation.

And on the phone

The app keeps an offline copy of the records it has seen, so it works without a signal, plus a stored sign-in session. Both live in the app's private storage.

On Android, we switch off Google's app backup and device-to-device transfer for PartyBook. That is deliberate: the offline copy holds customers' names, numbers and addresses, and we would rather it never be copied off the phone by the operating system. Removing the app removes it.

Who it is shared with

Nobody. We do not sell, rent or share your records, and we do not use them to build profiles or train anything.

There is no analytics in PartyBook. No advertising, no tracking, no crash-reporting service, no third-party SDK beyond what is listed here. The app does not know how you use it.

Things the app does that look like sharing, and are not

  • The Call button on a pending payment opens your phone's own dialler with the customer's number filled in. It does not place a call, and the number goes nowhere but your dialler.
  • Printing or sharing a bill creates a PDF on your phone. Where it then goes — a printer, WhatsApp, email — is your choice, and after that it is outside PartyBook.
  • The request forms for a hotel account or a PIN reset open your own email app with the details filled in. Nothing is sent until you press send in that app.

How long it is kept

For as long as the business wants it. Order history is the point of the product, and a cancelled order is deliberately kept rather than deleted so the record survives with its bill number intact.

We take daily backups of the database and keep them for seven days, so an accidental deletion can be undone.

A business can ask us to delete its hotel and everything in it, by emailing partybook.support@gmail.com from an administrator. We will confirm before doing it, because it cannot be undone once the backups age out.

Security, stated plainly

  • Records are readable only by accounts belonging to that hotel, enforced on the server and not only in the app.
  • PINs and cancellation codes are stored one-way, as described above.
  • The app carries no administrative key. Changing another person's credential happens on a server, only for an administrator of that same hotel, and never for a hotel's only administrator.
  • What we do not claim: a PIN is six digits, and a phone left unlocked on a counter is a phone anybody can pick up. PartyBook is built for a shared counter handset, and its sign-in reflects that. Treat the phone as the key.

Children

PartyBook is business software, not intended for or directed at children, and accounts are issued to businesses rather than to individuals who sign themselves up.

Your requests

A business using PartyBook can ask us for a copy of its records, a correction, or deletion of its hotel. Email partybook.support@gmail.com from an administrator's address and we will reply within a reasonable time.

If you are a customer of a shop that uses PartyBook, ask the shop — see the first section.

Changes to this policy

If it changes in a way that matters, we will change the date at the top and tell the businesses using PartyBook by email.
